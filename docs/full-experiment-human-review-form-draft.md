Read the bill and summary once for these three concerns only:

1. **Factual accuracy:** Does the summary assert something that conflicts with, or is not established by, the supplied bill text?
2. **Material omission:** Does it leave out an operative change, exception, condition, amount, deadline, or other detail whose absence would materially mislead a reader about the bill?
3. **Substantive meaning:** Does its wording change who must act, what is required or permitted, the scope of a rule, or another legally significant point?

Record each observed problem below. A problem may fit more than one concern; do not count it twice. For a material omission, quote the missing source passage and write `not present` for the summary passage. For an unsupported claim with no source passage, write `not established in supplied bill text` and briefly explain the missing support. This is a short issue log, not a claim-by-claim score sheet.

Save and timestamp this form before looking at Astra's output. After all six forms are complete, note any illustrative disagreement with Astra separately; do not change the original notes silently or calculate inter-rater reliability. This one-researcher, three-bill check can uncover examples of evaluator errors or important omissions. It cannot establish expert validity, estimate population error rates, or prove that unflagged summaries are accurate.
